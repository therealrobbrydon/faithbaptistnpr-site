Devotion

DEAR READER,

Thank you for joining us in this 21 Days of Prayer. We pray that this devotional booklet will serve as a source of encouragement and blessing during your time with the Lord. Each devotion is designed to guide you deeper into your relationship with God, reflecting on His many names and attributes.

In addition to contributions from our pastoral team, we are excited to share devotions written by several members of our church family. Each individual was carefully selected by the pastoral team and assigned to write about each subject as we Seek Him. These devotions were written under the guidance and supervision of our pastoral staff, to enrich your time in prayer.

Please keep in mind that these devotions are not meant to be authoritative or instructional, but rather to encourage and uplift you. We hope that these reflections will bless you, helping you focus your heart on prayer and drawing you closer to our Savior.

Daily Devotions for 21 Days of Prayer

November 2nd - Quieting the Noise

“Be still, and know that I am God.” — Psalm 46:10

 

Life is noisy.

Psalm 46 describes earth giving way, mountains falling into the sea, and waters roaring and foaming. Yet in the middie of all that noise, God says, “Be still, and know that I am God.”

Being still does not mean our problems disappear. It means quieting the noise of life so that we may know He is God.

Sometimes the noise is around us—responsibilities, schedules, phones, television, and endless activity.

Sometimes the loudest noise is within us—worries about tomorrow, regrets from yesterday, and problems demanding our attention today. God invites us to become still.

Not because stillness itself is the goal, but because He is.

When Elijah encountered God, He was not in the wind, earthquake, or fire. Elijah heard Him in a low whisper. Perhaps we need less noise and more margin—moments when we deliberately turn aside from life’s distractions, open God’s Word, quiet our hearts, and seek Him.

The Psalm begins, “God is our refuge and strength,” and ends, “The LORD Almighty is with us.” Between those two truths comes His invitation: Be still. Quiet the noise. Seek Him. Know that He is God.

 

- Mark Burritt

November 3rd - The Power of Desire

Psalm 10:17

“Lord, You have heard the desire of the humble; You have prepared their heart; You will cause Your ear to hear.”

Augustine said, “Sin comes when we take a perfectly natural desire or longing or ambition and try desperately to fulfill it without God.” Where we take our desires determines the fruit of our lives and the effectiveness of our prayers.

Psalm 10 teaches us three truths. First, we must acknowledge God as Lord over our desires. Proverbs 3:5–6 reminds us to trust Him rather than lean on our own understanding.

Second, our prayers must be characterized by humility. Pride leads to self-reliance, but James 4:6 tells us that God gives grace to the humble.

Third, God continually prepares our hearts to align our desires with His. Philippians 2:13 reminds us that God works in us both to will and to do His good pleasure. Just as we depended on Christ for salvation, we must depend daily on the Holy Spirit and God’s Word to transform our desires.

Jesus declared in John 4:34 that His food was to do the Father’s will and finish His work. When our desires align with God’s will, we can pray confidently, knowing He hears us and answers according to His purposes (1 John 5:14–15).

-Amy Bush

November 4th - Uncluttering the Soul

It has been a typical day filled with clutter— conversations, meetings, decisions, problems to solve, family time, and countless texts, emails, and calls. Finally, the day grows quiet, and I have time to meet with the Lord. As I reflect, I recognize the thoughts, concerns, and distractions competing for my attention. It is time to unclutter my soul.

I begin with David’s prayer in Psalm 139:23-24: “Search me, God, and know my heart; test me and know my anxious thoughts. See if there is any offensive way in me, and lead me in the way everlasting.” I invite God to reveal my anxieties, expose my sin, and clarify what needs to change.

Then I remember Psalm 46:10: “Be still and know that I am God.” I can stop striving, release my grip on what I cannot control, and rest in who He is. 1 Peter 5:7 reminds me to cast all my anxiety on Him because He cares for me. Psalm 55:22 assures me that as I cast my burdens on the Lord, He will sustain me. I was never meant to

carry life’s burdens alone.

God’s Word brings renewal. Psalm 19:7 declares that His law refreshes the soul, while Psalm 23:3 reminds me that He restores my soul and guides me in right paths for His name’s sake.

As I end my day, Isaiah 26:3 reminds me that God keeps in perfect peace those whose minds are steadfast because they trust in Him.

Prayer: Lord, thank You for meeting me in the clutter and drawing me back to Your truth. Quiet my heart, restore my soul, and help me rest in You. In Jesus’ name, amen.

 

- Mike Bush

November 5th - Honesty in His Presence

There were seasons when I didn't have pretty words for God. I had sighs, tears, and questions I was afraid to ask out loud. Then I recalled saints of old, like Hannah who poured out her soul to the LORD (1 Samuel 1:15), like Job who voiced his suffering (Job 7:11), like David who asked, “How long, O LORD?” (Psalm 13:1)

My honesty in the presence of God brought comfort and confidence in my prayers. He doesn't rebuke us for our honesty. He meets us with mercy, patience, provision, and His presence. Because I am fully known and loved, I can approach the throne of grace with confidence (Hebrews 4:16).

And He hears. “The righteous cry out, and the LORD hears them” (Psalm 34:17). “I love the LORD, for he heard my voice and my pleas for mercy. Because he inclined his ear to me, therefore I will call on him as long as I live” (Psalm 116:1-2).

So what are you holding back? Pour out your heart to God today. He is near and listening.

Prayer: Father, thank you that you hear me and know my every sigh. Help me come to you honestly, trusting your mercy and presence. Amen.

 

- Joanne Figueroa

November 6th - Turning Away to Turn Toward

Hosea 14:1-9

 

We tend to think of repentance primarily in terms of what we need to stop doing. Stop the sin. Break the habit. Walk away from what is wrong. While all of that is certainly necessary, biblical repentance is about more than what we turn away from. It is about Who we are turning toward.

The book of Hosea is the story of God's unfailing love toward an unfaithful people. Israel had wandered from God and given its affection to other things. Yet after exposing their sin and warning of its consequences, Hosea ends with an incredible invitation: “O Israel, return unto the LORD thy God.”

That's the heart of repentance. God isn't simply calling His people to leave their sin; He is calling them to come home to Him. Their sin had created distance, but His mercy invited their return. Hosea even tells them what to bring: “Take with you words, and return to the LORD.” The same is true for us. Sometimes we know something isn't right in our relationship with God. Our hearts have grown cold, our attention has wandered, or something else has taken a place that belongs to Him.

As we continue to seek the Lord during these 21 days, don't ask only, what do I need to turn away from? Ask also, what is keeping me from drawing closer to the Lord? Today, turn away— but make sure you are turning toward Him.

 

- Pastor Micah

November 7th - The Posture of Humility

In the movie “Catch Me If You Can,” Leonardo DiCaprio portrays a real-life story of a man, Frank Abagnale, who was a master of disguise. For years, he was successful at disguising himself as a pilot, doctor, and lawyer. While some things can be disguised, the inward condition of the heart cannot. In the Bible, the Lord told Samuel, “Man looks on the outward appearance, but the Lord looks on the heart.” 1 Samuel 16:7

Humility is one thing that can be easily disguised or misunderstood. We must recognize that it is not low self-worth, saying, “woe is me,” or being a pushover. The posture of humility can only be realized by being intently aware of our dependency on God, our surrender of pride to serve others, and looking to Him only.

Some measures of humility; how teachable we are, how we help others without expectation of return, how quickly we admit our mistakes, and how quickly we forgive the mistakes of others. For the Christian, humility is a discipline that requires effort. Thanking God, confessing sin, swallowing our pride, listening and helping others are daily exercises. We have clear instruction in Philippians 2:3: “Do nothing out of selfish ambition or vain conceit. Rather, in humility value others above yourselves.”

 

- Faith Brooks

November 8th - First Love Restored

In Revelation 2, Jesus commended the church at Ephesus for its good works but warned that in their zeal to accomplish works for God, they had lost their love for Him. Instead of loving God with their whole heart, soul and mind (Matthew 22:37) they’d begun to worship the works of their hands.

When David was anointed king, God told Samuel that while people may look at outward appearances, He looks at the heart. What did God see in David— A man after His own heart. Have you ever chased someone in a game of tag? You fix your eyes on that person and follow hard after them—turning when they turn— reaching and straining with everything in you trying to touch them. David’s heart was like that, single focused toward God—until he took his eyes off God to follow after something else.

Both David and the Ephesian church were called to repentance. On the surface their sins seem very different, but the root issue was the same — they turned their hearts toward lesser things. Let us not be guilty of this, instead fixing our eyes on Jesus, let’s run the race set before us, reaching to lay hold of Jesus, for He is worthy of our love.

 

- Jodie Sewall

November 9th - Hidden in The Word

"Every word of God is pure; He is a shield to those who take refuge in Him." - Proverbs 30:5

 

David spent a good portion of his life hiding from his enemies. He hid in caves, forests, rock gorges, and even enemy strongholds. David also understood that his best defense against his greatest enemy, the devil, was in being hidden in the truths of God’s Word. Jesus also employed this strategy when being tempted by the devil during his 40 days and nights in the wilderness (Matt 4).

David’s familiarity with the Word gave him an intimate knowledge of who God was, how He operated in the present, His faithfulness in the past, and what He promised to do in the future. (Psalm 143;5; Psalm 119:140) David gave himself wholeheartedly to the disciplines of seeking and waiting upon the guidance of God through prayer.

In the midst of life’s greatest challenges, God’s Word supplied the refreshment, wisdom, and hope that he needed to persevere. (Psalm 19:7) Meditating on God’s Word moved his anxious thoughts from earthly fear to heavenly security and worship. (Psalm 32:7) It also was the convicting and purifying agent that kept him from sins that would hinder his prayer life and get him into trouble. (Psalm 119:9; 119:98-99)

Knowing and aptly applying God’s Word helps to align our hearts with His will. It provides us with insight to help us to avoid the traps of our enemy and to prompt intimate communion with our Heavenly Father.

 

- Amy Bush

November 10th - The Whisper of Prayer

Have you ever been in a crowded room and wanted to say something, but didn’t dare say it out loud, so you whispered it to the person next to you? Maybe it was something funny, a disagreement, or a question. Either way, you felt more comfortable sharing it with one person rather than everyone.

Do we know God like this? Do we neglect the whisper of prayer? While praying through a list is important, can we also turn to Him in a crowded room and whisper? Do we talk to God as if He’s really there, even though no one else can see Him? Maybe like Hannah? 1 Samuel 1:13 “...she spake in her heart; only her lips moved, but her voice was not heard...”

Or are we ready, like Nehemiah, to look to Heaven & ask for help? Nehemiah 2:4 “Then the king said unto me, For what dost thou make request? So I prayed to the God of heaven.”

God wants us to have that type of relationship with Him. He wants us to know Him privately so that when we are in public, we won’t be ashamed to whisper His name. Matthew 6:6 “...pray to thy Father which is in secret...”

 

- Pastor Chad

November 11th - Intentional Stillness

Psalm 46:10

 

Written against a backdrop where King Hezekiah and Judah seemed trapped before the unstoppable Assyrian Empire, Psalm 46:10 calls God’s people to a holy pause: “Be still, and know that I am God.”

Hezekiah knew the pressure of fear. When Assyria threatened Judah, being still was the last thing on Hezekiah’s mind. He tried to lean on his alliance with Egypt. When that failed, he gathered silver from the house of the LORD and even stripped all the gold from the temple doors to pay the enemy. Before he rested in God’s power, he tried to purchase peace!

How often do we do the same? We surrender what is sacred to manage what is frightening. We exhaust our plans, resources, words, and strength, in a hopeless quest to quiet our anxiety. Yet God calls us to intentional stillness, to cease striving long enough to remember who He is.

So ask yourself: What am I tempted to strip away because I am afraid? Where am I paying for peace instead of trusting God for deliverance? Stillness makes room to see Him work!

Prayer: Lord, teach me to be still, trust Your power, and wait for You. Amen.

 

- Andy Mater

November 12th - Seeking Him in Community

Acts 2:42 - “And they continued stedfastly in the apostles’ doctrine and fellowship, and in breaking of bread, and in prayers.”

 

The definition of community is “a group of people who share a sense of place, identity, interest, or background.” Guess what? FBC is our community! Each member of this body of believers has the commonality of our faith in Jesus Christ as our Lord and Savior. Therefore, being a child of God is our identity and what we all share.

The bible is filled with ways to be involved in community: Gal. 6:2 “Bear ye one another’s burdens”; Heb. 10:24 “Provoke unto love and to good works”; John 13:34 “Love one another; as I have loved you” — some of these are easy, some are not.

Coming to church on Sunday morning is great and needful. But, are you digging in the Word with others who are hungry for the things of God? Are you serving alongside one another to make His name known in our city? Are you intentionally encouraging a fellow brother or sister in Christ as they struggle?

Prayer: Lord, may we as a church, grow together in service to You. May we love each other as You have loved us. May we always keep You at the center of our lives, in all we say and do.

 

- Bekah Wallace

November 13th - The Sacrifice of Praise

Hebrews 13:15

 

A sacrifice of praise is choosing to worship God when it costs us something. It doesn't mean pretending our pain isn't real. It means coming to the Lord with raw honesty, remembering His faithfulness, and choosing to trust Him. We praise God not because of our circumstances, but because of who He is.

Praise becomes a sacrifice when we offer it in hard times, not just easy ones, and not in our own strength, but through Christ in us. We give thanks for what can never be taken away: our salvation, His presence, His promises.

Paul and Silas, beaten and imprisoned, prayed and sang hymns at midnight (Acts 16:16-40). They lived out Psalm 107:22, offering thanksgiving and telling of His works with joy.

So, dear brother and sister, when life feels unbearable, lift your eyes above your circumstances. Offer Him the fruit of your lips (Hebrews 13:15), declaring that He is worthy, the Alpha and Omega (Revelation 22:13), even in your darkest hour.

Prayer: Lord, You are worthy. Even in my darkest hour, I choose to praise You. Help me trust You, remember Your faithfulness, and fix my eyes on You. Thank You for my salvation, Your presence, and Your promises. Be glorified in my life. In Jesus’ name, Amen.

 

- Joanne Figueroa

November 14th - Fasting for His Fullness

Joel 2:12-13

 

Most of us don't like going without anything. We live in a world where nearly anything we want can be available within minutes. We get hungry, we eat. We want something, we order it. When we feel uncomfortable, we look for relief. Maybe that's one reason the spiritual discipline of fasting feels so foreign to us.

In Joel 2, God calls His people to “turn ye even to me with all your heart, and with fasting, and with weeping, and with mourning.” But the next verse makes clear that God isn't primarily interested in an outward religious exercise: “Rend your heart, and not your garments.”

The people were accustomed to outward demonstrations of sorrow, including tearing their clothing. But God wanted something deeper. He wanted their hearts. True repentance had to be more than an outward display; it required an inward work of grace. That's important when we fast. Fasting isn't about proving how spiritual we are, nor is it a way of convincing God to give us what we want. We willingly set something aside so that our attention and appetite can be redirected toward Him. Every hunger becomes a reminder: I need God more than I need this.

During these 21 days, don't fast simply to say you fasted. Let the absence create a greater awareness of your need for God. We willingly go without something temporary so that our hunger might be redirected toward something greater... the fullness found in Him.

 

- Pastor Micah

November 15th - Remembering His Faithfulness

"The Steadfast Love of the Lord never ceases; His mercies never come to an end, they are new every morning, great is your faithfulness." - Lamentations 3:22-23

 

The greatest joy in being a child of God is being able to look back and see His enduring faithfulness. In a world where everything is constantly changing, the promises we receive from God our Father and our Savior Jesus Christ never change. This unchanging nature is precisely what teaches us to trust and obey Him.

It was His steadfast love and mercy that placed our sins upon Jesus on the cross. Because of that ultimate sacrifice, we are forgiven, made new, and claimed as God's own. Our past sins will never be held against us because the righteousness of Jesus has been credited to our account. That is the definition of never-ending mercy.

He promises that He has a plan for our lives (Jeremiah 29:11) and that He is working all things together for our good and His glory (Romans 8:28). He says, "I will never leave you" (Hebrews 13:5), "My grace is sufficient" (2 Corinthians 12:9), and tells us to “Trust in the Lord with all your heart" (Proverbs 3:5-6). Great is His faithfulness.

 

- Janet Shanks

November 16th - Finding Him in the Ordinary

Philemon 15-16

 

Philemon 15-16 gives us a quiet glimpse of God’s providence. Paul writes that around Onesimus running from Philemon “for a while” so he might return “forever...no longer as a bondservant but more than a bondservant, as a beloved brother.” What looked like loss and offense became the circumstances God chose to display grace.

It reminds me of Genesis 50:20. God often works through ordinary, and sometimes painful, circumstances we would never choose. A wrong turn, a painful season, an unexpected conversation, or a verse remembered at just the right moment can become the place where Jesus meets us. Nothing is ordinary when Christ is involved!

Onesimus crossed Paul’s path in Rome, far from Colosse, yet Paul did not merely see a runaway. He saw a soul in need of Jesus. Grace looks beneath the mess and sees what only God can redeem.

So ask yourself: How do I see my circumstances? How do I see the people God brings across my path? In Christ, strangers become brothers, enemies become friends, and ordinary moments become appointments of grace!

Prayer: Lord, help me trust Your hand, see others with grace, and find You in the ordinary circumstances in my life. Amen.

 

- Andy Mater

November 17th - Trusting in the Dark

At some point in life, we will all turn a comer and find ourselves arriving at the same destination—the Valley of Tears.

(Ps. 84:5-7, John 16:33) Surprisingly the Bible says we can have peace, strength, and even joy as we make our pilgrimage through this dark valley. How is this possible?

Consider these thoughts as trail markers pointing you in the right direction when you arrive.

  • Know what you believe about God—so that when you arrive—you can confidently believe what you know.

If you wait until you enter the valley to decide what you believe about God, your perspective may get distorted by the darkness. The truth I believe is this— my All-Powerful God loves me.

  • Trust that your All-Powerful, loving God will only lead you to the valley if there is something of great value on the other side.

Is God refining or correcting you in preparation for greater works, as He did with Moses, Joseph, and David? Or perhaps God will display His glory to grow your faith—or the faith of those watching you—as He did with Job, Daniel, and the saints described in Hebrews 11.

Have you arrived? Keep walking. God loves you and is with you. Something of great value is just ahead.

 

- Jodie Sewall

November 18th - Obedience as Seeking

Micah 6:6-8

 

It is possible to spend a great deal of time seeking God without allowing that seeking to change the way we live. We can read our Bible, spend time in prayer, attend church, and participate in something like these 21 Days of Prayer and still resist what God is asking us to do.

Micah spoke to people who had fallen into that trap. God had redeemed them, but their relationship with Him had become little more than going through the motions of religion. They wondered what God wanted from them. More sacrifices? Thousands of rams? Rivers of oil? What could they possibly bring that would satisfy Him?

God's answer was remarkably practical: Do justly, love mercy, and walk humbly with thy God. In other words, there comes a point when seeking God requires us to get up from where we have been praying and obey what He has already shown us. Micah's emphasis wasn't merely talking about the right things, but actually walking them out.

Prayer isn't meant merely to give us information from God; it should transform us. If He exposes sin, confess it. If He tells you to forgive, forgive. If He puts someone on your heart, reach out. If He shows you something that needs to change, change it.

Perhaps today's prayer should be very simple: Lord, what have You already shown me that I still need to obey?

 

- Pastor Micah

November 19th - The Joy of His Presence

  • Revelation 22:13
  • Psalm 16

In Psalm 16, David reflects on finding refuge and safety in the Lord alone. He comes to God as his Master, understanding that all good things come from Him (vs. 2). While troubles seem to multiply for those who chase after other gods, David will take no part in offering sacrifices to them or even uttering their names (vs. 4). There is a sense of unhindered allegiance to God.

He says God is his inheritance, his ‘cup of blessing’ (vs. 5). The Lord guides him and counsels him. Even at night, his heart is instructed. He knows the Lord is always with him; therefore, he will not be shaken. The whole psalm crescendos in verse 11: “You make known to me the path of life; in your presence there is fullness of joy; at your right hand are pleasures forevermore.”

I have found in my own life that it’s through resting, reflecting, stillness and worship that I can see the reality that all I have is because of the Lord. What joy can be found apart from Him? Colossians 1 tells us that all things are held together by Him. May we find joy in the presence of our Creator, Sustainer, Comforter, and Savior.

 

- Lexi Reinig

November 20th - Seeking His Face, Not His Hand

Psalms 27:8 - "When thou saidst, Seek ye my face; my heart said unto thee, Thy face, LORD, will I seek."

 

It is hard to go through trials and tribulation, but one of the best byproducts of this is learning to seek God’s face. Unfortunately, we don’t always seek God’s face during trials, do we? We usually seek Him for relief. If there’s anyone who knew what it felt like to face trials, it was David. Maybe one reason why David was considered “a man after God’s own heart” is because of how many trials he endured.

But what about you? What are you looking for when you seek God? Is it possible that God is allowing us to go through difficulty so we will learn seek His face like He said in v.8? There are two reasons why we need to seek God’s face:

1) To experience the goodness of the Lord for ourselves. Psalm 34:8 “O taste and see that the LORD is good...”

2) To show Christ to others. A trial is not only an opportunity to draw closer to God, but a chance to show the world a peace that passes all understanding.

 

- Pastor Chad

November 21st - Overflowing to Others

“Like the woman at the well, I was seeking For things that could not satisfy.

And then I heard my Savior speaking—"Draw from My well that never shall run dry."

Fill my cup, Lord; I lift it up, Lord;

Come and quench this thirsting of my soul.

Bread Of Heaven, feed mc till I want no more.

Fill my Cup, fill it up and make whole.”

 

This Old hymn on the story of the Samaritan woman’s encounter with Jesus (John 4:1-42) After that meeting, her life was never the same. Her new life overflowed with the message of Christ, and many were saved.

Similarly, the people of Thessalonica responded to the Good News. Paul commended them for their "work of faith, and labor of love, and patience " (l Thess. 1:2-8), The abundant life Jesus saved us for (John 10:10) should overflow with the Good News of the Gospel and love for everyone. Especially our brothers and sisters in Christ, as we anticipate Jesus’ soon return.

Prayer: “…May the Lord make you increase and abound in love for one another and for all, ..so that He may establish your hearts blameless in holiness before our God and Father at the coming of our Lord Jesus with all the His  saints." I Thess. 3:12

 

- Robin Bell

November 22nd - A Lifelong Pursuit

“Not that I have already obtained all this, or have already arrived at my goal, but I press on...” — Philippians 3:12

 

After years of knowing and serving Christ, Paul made a remarkable admission: “I have not arrived.”

Paul had preached the gospel, planted churches, suffered for Christ, and experienced God in extraordinary ways. Yet his desire remained: “I want to know Christ” (Philippians 3:10). Seeking Him was not merely how Paul began the Christian life. Seeking Him was the pursuit of his entire life.

The same should be true of us. No matter how long we have walked with Jesus, there is still more of Him to know. Yesterday’s encounters with Christ cannot substitute for seeking Him today. Nor should past failures keep us from pursuing Him now. Paul said, “Forgetting what is behind and straining toward what is ahead, I press on toward the goal.”

I recently heard this thought: “Heaven is not a destination; itis a motivation.” Perhaps we could say it this way: Heaven is the motivation for a lifelong pursuit of Christ. We never graduate from seeking Him.

So don’t coast on yesterday’s faith. Press on. Seek Him today. Tomorrow, seek Him again—until the day faith becomes sight.

 

- Mark Burritt
